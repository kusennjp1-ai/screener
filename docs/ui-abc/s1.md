  - A module already loaded is handed to `React.lazy` as a thenable that resolves at once, so the screen renders in the same

  - A render error inside `StockView` is not caught: it propagates exactly as on main.
  - The failure text mirrors the chart box's (including its 「再読み込みしてください」). It appears in no yardstick view (no

    after it the stock opens on its loading line.)

### Deviation from the order

The order names "a small error boundary". The failure box is reached without one: the lazy component's load resolves a failed
import to the box. Reasons:
- 実測: with a boundary in place of the resolved failure (a scratch edit of `StockScreen.jsx`, reverted, run with the phone
  case of `brief-lazy.spec.js` on the dev server), the box rendered but the page reported the caught import failure twice as
  uncaught page errors (「Failed to fetch dynamically imported module: …/StockView.jsx」), which the static specs count
  (`expect(errors).toEqual([])`). React's development build replays a failed render outside the boundary.
- A boundary would also catch real render errors of the screen, which main lets propagate.

The behaviour asked for (a failure box with a 44 px 再読み込み that reloads the page) is the same.

### 2. A tap on a stalled connection opens the stock on its loading line

Before (S1-P1): on a phone, a row tap whose stock chunk was not loaded navigated only when `loadStockView()` settled. On a
connection that neither delivers nor fails the chunk, the tap gave no visible response.

Now (`46aac2b7`): the tap still waits for the chunk, but at most `STOCK_TAP_WAIT_MS` = **400 ms** (`stockViewLoader.js`).
If the chunk has not settled by then, `open()` navigates anyway and the stock opens on the screen's existing loading line
(「銘柄データを読み込み中…」, the `Suspense` fallback; the bar names the stock), which gives way to the screen when the chunk
arrives, or to the failure box with its 再読み込み when it fails. The timer and the chunk's settling share one guard:
whichever comes first navigates, once (one history entry); a newer tap cancels the older one's timer, so only the last tap
opens; and nothing opens if the screen changed meanwhile. At >= 1024 px nothing changes (`open()` never waits there).

Why 400 ms:
- 実測, the wait for a chunk that is merely on its way: a probe on the S1-P1 head build (390, CPU 4x, the local server)
  that holds the stock chunk's request until the tap and releases it at the tap measured tap → navigation 54-111 ms
  (7 runs: 111, 62, 55, 71, 72, 57, 54; load 0.44 → 1.33). 400 ms leaves such a tap opening the whole screen.
- With Lighthouse's mobile network applied at the tap (150 ms round trip, 1.64 Mbps down) the same probe read 534-569 ms,
  but the local server sends the chunk uncompressed (68.9 kB); the published site sends it gzip-compressed (24.46 kB), which
  at that bandwidth is about 0.12 s of transfer plus the round trip and the run, about 0.3 s (computed, 未検証 on a real
  phone). A tap there opens the whole screen; on a slower or stalled network it opens on the loading line at 400 ms.
- 400 ms keeps a stalled tap's visible response within about the time people still read as an immediate answer; a shorter
  bound would make more ordinary cold taps open on a loading frame first.
- In normal use the bound is rarely reached: the chunk is prefetched while the data loads (section 1), so a tap finds it
  loaded.

Deliberate test change (`brief-lazy.spec.js`, "before the chunk has landed: the tap waits for it, then opens the whole
screen"):
- Old: the chunk held for 600 ms after the tap, then the URL still `#/brief`, then release, the whole screen, no loading
  frame, back to Today.
- New (`46aac2b7`, then `8fa61646`): the chunk released right after the tap (well inside the bound); then the whole screen;
  no empty stock frame and no loading line before the bound; back to Today. `8fa61646` dropped the two checks between the
  tap and the release (the address still `#/brief`, Today's first row visible): they ran while the 400 ms timer did and could
  fail on a slow runner; a tap that navigates at once is still caught by the no-loading-line-before-the-bound check.
- Why: with the bound, a 600 ms hold now opens the loading line at 400 ms by design (that case is the new stalled test).
  On the dev server the released chunk settled inside the bound in three diagnostic runs (no loading frame at all), so the
  case still exercises the wait.

New tests (`brief-lazy.spec.js`, 8 in all now):
- "on a stalled connection: the tap opens the stock on its loading line at the bound, then the screen": the chunk held; the
  stock address and the loading line after the tap, the bar naming the stock; the loading line no earlier than the bound and
  less than the bound + 300 ms after the tap (the slack is the page transition's capture frame on a loaded CI machine); after
  release the screen and the chart, no empty stock frame; one 戻る returns to Today (one history entry).
  The ceiling (bound + 300 ms) is a wall-clock assert. It held in 10 of 10 CI-faithful repeats in the S1-P1b verification, and
